# 3 for Kiitos — definition, v0 (provisional)

Dated 2026-10-21. This file is frozen: a change is a new file (`DEFINITION-v1.md`),

**Status: v0, provisional.** Used by one person in one friendly place, and given a desk
screen for offensive meanings, not a clearance. Listed below: what is not yet known.

## The sign

Thumb, index and middle finger extended and spread apart. Ring and little finger folded.
The back of the hand faces the person being thanked, so they see a **3**.

- **Spread.** The index and middle finger never touch (touching reads as a finger gun).
- **Upright, back of the hand to the viewer.** The one canonical orientation.
- **Held, not aimed.** Raised in view, never pointed at a person.
- **A small wave is allowed** for emphasis. The still shape is the sign.
- **Palm out is not the sign.** It turns the shape into a letter *k*, and that shape already
  means something else to some people. Do not use it, and do not teach it.

## What it says

- **3.** Thumb, index and middle finger make a 3, the 3 in Heart3ank, the wordmark of
  HeartBank®.
- **Kiitos.** Finnish for *thank you*, with *kiitti* its sister word.
- **Thank you.** Where you would nod, mouth the words, or give a quick wave.

## Where the shape comes from

The shape is the number 3 in American Sign Language, and in several countries it is how three
is counted on one hand. We give it a second meaning and credit the Deaf community whose
language it already belongs to. ASL has its own sign for *thank you*; this does not replace it.

## What is not yet known

- Palm out collides with the Serbian three-finger salute, which many Bosniaks and Croats read
  as hostile. Palm-in is the only orientation here for that reason.
- No gang-awareness source has reviewed it. Treat it as unvetted until a review is recorded.
- Reading by country is incomplete; the sources were thin. Cambodia in particular is a gap.
- Adoption is untested.

## Use

The gesture is free for anyone to use. It carries no trademark and none is planned.
