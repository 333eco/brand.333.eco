B-Watermark℠

What a signed picture can carry, so that anyone who meets it anywhere can get from the picture to its page. It has five parts, and two looks with no code. Each one is the signer's choice, and each is an address or a drawing. None is a claim.

Part one

The mark you can't see

A pattern written into the colors by Adobe's open TrustMark. It carries one number: the address of a page. It usually survives resizing, posting and a screenshot. It is made and read on the person's own device.

  • Never a claim. It says where to look. Anyone can write any number into any picture, so the page it opens shows the picture that was signed, and you compare.
  • Never listed. A mark's address has twelve characters and is published nowhere in plain words, so nobody can walk through every marked picture.

Parts two and three

The B-QR, and its four positions

A QR code has three markers. The middle one of the three stands in one of four corners, and where it stands says what kind of address the code holds. The drawing code reads the address and chooses; a person never does.

  • Symmetric means a B-Link. At the point and at the notch the code mirrors along the heart's own line. A B-Link opens only pages of ours.
  • Every other address is ours too. The drawing code refuses any address outside our own domains, whoever asks it to draw.
  • Not a proof of who drew it. This recipe is public, so anyone can draw any position. Look at the address your camera shows.
  • Any angle reads. Cameras don't care which way a code is turned, so every code printed before still works.

Part four

B-Autograph℠

In the Open B-QR, inside a person's own picture, the bottom lobe carries their own hand in place of our ™. Draw here with a finger or a mouse, and every heart on this page takes it.

  • Strokes, never a picture of a hand. Points on a small pad. No timing and no pressure is read. Here nothing is kept; in the app it stays on the device.
  • Never a legal or bank signature. Anyone can copy what is in a picture, and a drawing doesn't show who someone is.
  • Drawn, or typed. A typed name is set in a one-stroke script (qr/script.ts, Latin letters only), as strokes on the same pad.
  • Never on a picture made with AI. And the person chooses how it was made before adding one; nothing is filled in for them.
  • Never in our own codes. They keep the ™, which names the B-Emblem™ and shows which way is up.

Part five

B-Fingerprint℠

A short fingerprint of the picture, never of a person. The picture is seen as 36 cells, and each dot in the top lobe says whether a cell is lighter or darker than the middle. The cells the heart stands on are left out. Below, this page reads the dots from the pixels it has just drawn.

  • It tells a copied heart apart. Measured on 97 pictures, 194 posted corners: the dots read whenever the code did, at 1080 and 720 pixels, and never called the right picture different. A heart copied onto another varied photo was told apart 144 times of 152.
  • It can't see an edit. Thirty-six cells are coarse. Two pictures with the same rough layout look the same to it.
  • It is not a secret, and not a lock. Someone can redraw a heart for another picture. It catches a copied one. The page the code opens shows the signed picture, and that decides.
  • Nothing about anyone. No serial number, no name, no claim and no key.

Part six

Two looks with no code

For an artist who wants only a hint: a faint heart in a corner, or the B-Symbol℠, a tiny B-Emblem beside their own hand, as a ™ stands beside a name. Neither carries an address, so each comes only with the mark you can't see, which does. Both take the hand you draw or type above.

  • Never without the mark you can't see. It is written into the same picture and read back before the press; where it doesn't hold, the look is refused.
  • No hand on a picture made with AI. So no B-Symbol there. A faint heart with no hand may stand on one.
  • Centered in the heart's bottom section, from the notch where its lobes meet down to its bottom edge. The heart is sized to fit the hand, and both its fill and its edge are 10% white.
  • The ink is the person's. Or whichever reads better on the picture under it. The B-Symbol stands raised, like a ™, or lowered.
  • Still never a claim. A faint heart can be pasted on any picture. Only the page the mark leads to answers.

The pixels hold an address and 36 cells. That is not enough to check a signature. A receipt or a proof travels in the file's own data, where most networks remove it when a picture is posted; the picture's marks are what is left, and they lead back. A receipt shows the file was signed. A proof, once the record has joined the log, shows the record is kept.

Where it is made

A person adds it when they sign a picture at origin.333.eco, and reads one at Check a file. Every part starts switched off. The drawing code and the fingerprint's reader are in this package; the names belong to their owner, and the vendoring notes say what you may do with the files.
